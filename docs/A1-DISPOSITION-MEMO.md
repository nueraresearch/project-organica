# A1 disposition memo — redesign or park

**ID:** `A1-DISPOSITION-1`

**Status:** `DRAFT — OWNER DECISION REQUIRED; NO FREEZE, NO GRANT, NO RUN`

**This memo records the choice and its criteria; it does not choose.** The manifest remains `REGISTERED DESIGN — NOT FROZEN, NOT RUN, NO GRANT`; no target is frozen, no seed is reserved and no A1 cell has run.

## 1. The decision

Should A1 be **redesigned as a new protocol**, or **parked**? The current design is blocked below the adopted C1 floor. The choice is whether to fund a successor that can reach it, or close the branch without claiming an answer.

## 2. Fixed evidence

- **C1:** the candidate ceiling is **31–44** rows per seed. Changing N, replacing N4 with N20, or reranking targets cannot raise it. The 112-row stream is **382 candidate + 286 reverse + 4 extra** rows, collapsing to **384 unique undirected edges**; reverse orientation is duplication, not a rescue.
- **K is limiting at the shipped caps:** A is available on **281/382**, K on **228/382**. **95** rows have A without K, **42** have K without A, **59** have neither. The shortfall to 60 is **16–29 rows** per seed; K is lower than A on five seeds and tied on one. The cap-120 counterfactual shows this is a **first** constraint, not the whole system: A then becomes the marginal binder on 18/18 seeds.
- **No cheap local lever:** K rises from **31.8%** in the lowest cycle-36 U quartile to **89.1%** in the highest, but the high-U half has only **28–31 edges** per seed. **154/382** rows never form K; only **28/228** available rows form it after cycle 60. Tag rates span **44.7–77.1%** pooled but **0–100%** by seed; candidate/reverse pairs agree **97.2%**; the universe is near-regular. Tags, orientation and degree are not credible levers on these six seeds.
- **C2/C8 are a selection problem, conditionally:** the pool has **23–36 A&K-available rows** per seed and a 20-row matching exists on **6/6**; the current N4 block supplies only **12/24** competent rows. A competent N20 block is constructible but **pre-treatment rather than future-blind** to the no-arm baseline; it changes the estimand and **still does not repair C1**.

## 3. Redesign branch — requirements

A successor is admissible only if **all** requirements are met before any target freeze. The table compares what each branch changes.

| Scope | Redesign | Park |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 stream | New independent universe or seed block; no-arm preflight shows **≥60 K-available independent rows per seed**. | Close the current **31–44** stream; do not retry target selection. |
| K rate / size | At 63–64 edges, **93.8–95.2% K**; a larger universe lowers the rate: 80 edges → **75%**, 96 → **62.5%**, 120 → **50%**. | No K repair; park does not claim K is unavailable in principle. |
| Targets | If C2/C8 remain in scope, **20 competent targets per seed** under a declared baseline A+K screen, with the estimand change recorded. | No new target selection; C2/C8 are **not adjudicated**. |
| Estimator / floor | Keep the **0.95 / 60** rule unless separately reopened as a scientific change. | No change. |
| Universe / scorer | `run_cell`, selection, result assembly and manifest use **one edge universe**; the N20/N4 mismatch is removed. | Fix independently if desired; not a condition of park. |
| Governance | New protocol, freeze, seed reservation and dated grant; current acts are not reused. | **No freeze, no grant, no run.** |
| Cost | Fund the preflight, then a new campaign only if it passes. | No new campaign; preserve the existing artifacts. |

**Stop rule:** if the no-arm preflight cannot show 60 K-available rows on every seed, **do not freeze, do not grant and do not run**. Return to disposition; do not relax the floor, add non-competent rows, or select a more favourable seed subset after seeing the preflight.

A redesign may target **C1 only**, but must declare C2/C8 **not adjudicated**; it may not claim C2/C8 coverage without the competent-target protocol.

## 4. Park branch — criteria

Park is defensible if any of the following holds:

- No fresh universe or seed block reaches 60 K rows without selecting seeds or edges on the same no-arm baseline that judges them.
- The only route to 60 is changing the estimator or lowering the floor without independent justification — a different study, not a repair.
- The competent-target policy is rejected as too large an estimand change, and C1-only redesign is not worth the cost.
- The universe/scorer inconsistency and governance work cannot be closed within a bounded protocol, or the expected information gain does not justify a new freeze/grant cycle.

**Park means** recording A1 as **not run / not identified under the current design**, with C1 as the blocking gate. It **does not mean treatment failure**, a negative C2/C8 result, or evidence that the effect is absent. Preserve the ceiling, dedupe, baseline, target-viability and K-decomposition records; do not re-select targets or reinterpret the unrun cells.

## 5. Decision rule

| Condition | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh universe/seed block passes **≥60 K on every seed**, with one scorer and a pre-registered competence policy if C2/C8 are in scope. | **Redesign**, then freeze, grant and run under the new protocol. |
| No such preflight exists; only a floor/estimator change or selection on the judging baseline remains. | **Park**, without a treatment claim. |
| The preflight is not built, but a bounded path to it exists. | **Hold**; sign nothing and fund only the preflight. |

**Current default:** hold. The N4 and N20 freeze acts remain unsigned, the grant is absent and the manifest is unfrozen. Holding is not a third disposition; it is the required state until one branch is chosen.

## 6. What would settle it

The decisive artifact is the **no-arm successor preflight**, now exercised on a predeclared fresh seed block: the block `20650101–20650106` fails **0/6**, with C1 ceilings **40, 37, 46, 42, 31, 38** and a best gap of **14** rows. It was fixed without K screening and was not selected on high K, so this is not a selected-on-the-baseline result; a one-seed timing probe did not alter the block. The preflight reports independent edges, K-available count and C1 ceiling per seed, whether 20 competent targets can be matched if C2/C8 are in scope, and the exact universe used by the runner and result layer. This one block does not clear 60; it also does not prove that no fresh block could. A second predeclared candidate, a **28-tag larger universe**, cleared the structural target at **158–172** independent edges but failed worse at C1 **23, 40, 42, 55, 47, 37**, with K diluted to **23–55** and matching below 20 on four seeds. The K dilution report shows why the larger block failed: candidate edges rose **383 → 1000**, but K-available rows rose only **242 → 244**; old-old K density fell **63.2% → 18.9%**, so the loss is not merely the new tags. Under the stop rule, the tested same-world and larger-world constructions both fail; **park** is the defensible default unless a different predeclared construction **specifically explains and overcomes K dilution**, not merely adds edges. The K-supply discriminator sharpens the next step: prediction caps **20/40/80** move A **271 → 353 → 498** but K stays **240 → 244 → 244**, so prediction-cap is not the K bound; support is real (**10.7% → 40.9%** across support bins) but not sufficient (matched-support gap **25.0 pp**), so the next diagnostic must target the carrier path directly. The absolute-K predictor join adds a caution: across **18** seeds, no pre-run feature beats the mean baseline out of world, and `A_available` — the best model at **4.65** K rows — is itself a post-run output, only **0.16** better than baseline. Total K is **seed-specific** under these summaries, so another structural predictor join is not a mechanism. The carrier-supply trace names the window: across **18** seeds, **1,765** candidate edges yield **714** candidate K, but **1,004** loop-eligible candidates never become K. Every loop-carried K ranks **1–15** in the engine's loop order; every eligible non-K ranks **16+** (median **49**). The builder sees **1,421,231** possible 2-/3-cycles and returns **26,932** (**1.90%**), and the 15-loop cap binds on **81.2%** of cycles. Candidate K stays **228/242/244** across **382/383/1,000** candidates, while engine-wide directed K sums to **3,548** against **714** candidate K. K dilution is therefore a fixed output-window effect, not an edge shortage. The counterfactual then raises that window: the same **18** worlds at loop caps **15/30/60/120** give candidate K **714 → 1,001 → 1,294 → 1,498**, with **10** edges reshuffled out in total. At cap 120 the 16-tag blocks reach **382/383** candidate K and the 28-tag block reaches **733/1,000**. The window therefore causes the ceiling in direction, but it is **not a fixed K quota**: an **8×** cap buys **2.1×** candidate K, and the 28-tag world still loses a quarter of its candidates to the window. Strict verdict **MIXED** because the response is not lossless. The C1 counterfactual then shows what remains: at cap 120, A is the binding path on **18/18** seeds, K on **0**, and the marginal C1 ceiling passes only **2/18** (larger-28 `20660104` at **67**, `20660105` at **66**); the joint A∩K ceiling passes **0/18**, because those overlaps are **58** and **52**. The A edge set is identical at caps 15 and 120. Widening the loop window removes K as the C1 blocker, but C1 becomes prediction-path-limited; it does not make C1 viable alone. The prediction-cap counterfactual shows that path is movable: at loop cap 120, prediction caps **20/40/80** move A **744 → 913 → 1,129** while K stays **1,499 → 1,498 → 1,497**. Marginal C1 passes **0 → 2 → 6 /18**; joint C1 passes **0 → 0 → 4 /18**. All four joint passes are larger-28 (`20660101` **68**, `20660104` **77**, `20660105` **72**, `20660106` **70**); `20660102` (**56**) and `20660103` (**51**) fail on overlap, and both 16-tag blocks remain **0/6** (joint maxima **59** and **58**). Prediction capacity is a lever, but cap 80 is not a complete C1 repair. The shipped cap 112 is better: A reaches **1,269**, A∩K **1,150**, and joint C1 **8/18**. That is **2/12** in the 16-tag blocks (`20630103` 60, `20650104` 63; the other ten fail with max 59) and **6/6** in larger-28 (minimum 60). Cap 112 is the first condition where the marginal and joint counts agree: no passing seed loses its pass to overlap. The larger construction is therefore no longer C1-blocked at this cap pair, while the 16-tag A shortfall remains. The cap-112 decomposition localizes that shortfall: across the ten failing seeds, all **69** A-missing edges have support **>=5**, all are transition candidates, and **62/69** are `flip_q`. A rate is **97.8%** for `flip_p` versus **80.5%** for `flip_q`. Served A ranks are mostly top-20, but **14** A edges sit at 57–112. A diagnostic cap 1000 locates all 69 missing edges at ranks 65–189, but changes reinforcement, so this is a ranking/family constraint, not a raw support floor or a pure window cutoff. The cap-80 larger-28 overlap decomposition makes the analogous distinction on the K side: all six seeds clear A marginally (**75–91**), but K coverage removes the joint pass on two. `20660102` covers **56/75** A edges (A\K **19**); `20660103` covers **51/83** (A\K **32**). Only **4** and **9** of those uncarried A edges need K coverage to reach 60, while K totals are **124** and **105** against A **75** and **83**. K coverage is **74.7%** and **61.4%** on the failures versus at least **79.1%** on the four passes. The joint failure is therefore selective K omission, not K scarcity or A shortage; the K\A pools (**68/54**) give a prediction-side alternative repair. The selection trace identifies the two gates: all **104/104** A\K edges are eligible cycles ranked **121–848**, above the `MAX_LOOPS=120` entropy-ranked loop cut, with no cycle-strength failures and no dream carriers. On the A side, **254/338** K\A edges are transitions ranked **41–383**, outside the cap-80 `transition_budget=40`; **68** are below `PREDICTION_MIN_COUNT=5`, **16** never become candidates, and none is a cooccurrence-quota loss. The loss is two selection windows, not general support scarcity. The K-completeness test then separates a regime marker from the cap lever: all 16-tag rows have `K/candidates = 1.000` at caps 20/40/80/112, while larger-28 rows span **0.6105–0.8485**, a margin of **0.1515**. But larger-28 completeness is nearly static (**0.734 → 0.732**; maximum per-seed range **0.0121**) while joint passes move **0 → 0 → 4 → 6**, and at cap 80 the lowest-completeness pass (**0.6568**) is below the highest-completeness failure (**0.7425**). Completeness marks which overlap constraint is possible; it does not order joint success or explain the cap response. The response-curve fit sharpens the latter: a single shared `sqrt(cap)` curve plus a block offset predicts the 12 block-by-cap cells with LOO RMSE **0.0193** (max **0.0333**), better than block-specific sqrt slopes (**0.0209**), arbitrary per-cap effects (**0.0232**) or block-specific linear slopes (**0.0398**). Larger-28's relative offset is **−0.3512** against current-16, while fresh is **−0.0136**. The blocks differ mainly in baseline coverage, not response shape over this range. The offset-predictor join asks whether that baseline offset is a free block label or a candidate-space quantity: `log(candidate_edges)` reproduces the cap-0 offsets (**0.414** current, **0.413** fresh, **0.069** larger) with cell LOO **0.0184** and seed LOO **0.0667**, against **0.0193** / **0.0668** for three free block parameters, so one continuous term replaces two dummies. K completeness alone is **0.0189** / **0.0865** and adds no seed-level signal on top of size (**0.0669**); within larger-28 it correlates with A coverage only **0.079** while being **−0.866** collinear with candidate size. The offset is therefore summarized by candidate-space size, with K completeness retained as the regime marker rather than a second predictor. Nothing here intervenes on either quantity, and three blocks cannot distinguish a candidate-space law from a three-point coincidence. The holdout tests that summary against caps that were not fitted: predicting the same 18 seeds at caps **28/64/96** from the frozen fit gives RMSE **0.0139** (max **0.0237**), and at cap **128**, above the fitted range, **0.0142** (max **0.0223**) — both inside the fit's own LOO **0.0193** — so over this interval the curve interpolates and extrapolates alike. Two bounds apply: cap 128 is **84%** of current-16's saturation cap (**152**) and **80%** of fresh-16's (**160**), and the 16-tag cells already fall below the curve at 96 and 128 (fresh **−0.024**, current **−0.022**), so the fit is not admissible past those caps. The block holdout separates the two axes: holding out either 16-tag block leaves a bracketing pair and predicts at **0.0216** / **0.0197**, but holding out larger-28 leaves two blocks whose log candidate sizes differ by **0.0026**, so the size coefficient is unconstrained (**−5.17** against an intercept of **21.89**, predicted coverage **−4.33**) and that direction is **not identified**. The offset model therefore substitutes for the block label only inside the size range it was trained on; extrapolating to a larger candidate space needs a training block whose size varies. The turnover trace answers what the higher cap actually serves: A is **nested** from **1,129** to **1,269**, with **140** new and **0** dropped edges, and the new edges carry K at the retained rate — **1.000** on the K-complete 16-tag seeds and **0.812** against **0.791** on larger-28. The pooled rate falls (**0.893** vs **0.908**) only through composition, since the new edges are **57.1%** larger-28 against **44.1%** of retained; block-adjusted, they add **125** K-covered edges against **123.3** expected. The joint gain from **1,025** to **1,150** is therefore overlap, not extra A, and the cap is not diluting the overlap constraint. The density comparison adds the third candidate for the offset and cannot separate it from size: candidate edges per tag reaches cell LOO **0.0182** / seed LOO **0.0689**, against **0.0184** / **0.0667** for log candidate size, **0.0193** / **0.0668** for the free offset and **0.0189** / **0.0865** for K completeness. Because density is the candidate count divided by a block constant, the two correlate **1.000** within larger-28 and order the blocks identically (**4.15 / 3.98**, **4.16 / 3.99**, **5.12 / 5.95**), so three blocks cannot choose between counting candidates and measuring alphabet occupancy. Density is an equally admissible label, not a better one.

**Not evidence:** a decision memo. It runs no cell, freezes no target, reserves no seed, issues no grant and makes no treatment claim.
